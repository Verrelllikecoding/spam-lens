## Why each message is ambiguous

- **A01 (Security alert without a link):** Urgent scam-like wording, but it sends the reader to a trusted number and asks for nothing.
- **A02 (One-time code):** A normal transactional code, yet the same text appears in account-takeover attempts.
- **A03 (Possible wrong-number opener):** Could be a real contact or the opener of a trust-building scam. Nothing in the text decides it.
- **A04 (Family impersonation):** Sounds personal, and it is also the first step of a well-known family-impersonation scam.
- **A05 (Delivery notice with a link):** Couriers send this every day, and so do parcel scammers. Only the sender and link can separate them.
- **A06 (Spam words in a personal message):** Uses the words won, free, and promotion, but it is an invitation between friends.
- **A07 (Opted-in business promotion):** A legitimate business and possibly wanted by the recipient, yet still marketing.
- **A08 (Very short and urgent):** Too short to judge. It suits both a worried friend and a callback scam.
- **A09 (Too-good job offer):** Recruiters do text applicants, but high pay and moving the chat to WhatsApp are scam signs.
- **A10 (Billing receipt with a link):** A plain receipt that has the money amount and link typical of spam.
- **A11 (Charity appeal):** Real charities fundraise this way, and scammers copy the format after disasters.
- **A12 (Two-word question):** Contains a classic spam word, and has almost no other context.
